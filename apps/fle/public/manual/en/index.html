FLEonline Manual

日本語 · ← Back to the app

FLEonline is an app for writing your SOTA/POTA operating log on a phone in the field and turning it into files for SOTA, POTA and HAMLOG. This manual follows an ordinary SOTA activation, from preparation to generating the ADIF and other files. A reference of the FLE log syntax is at the end.

  2. Before you leave (PC): settings and plans
  3. Sync the PC and the phone
  4. On the summit: write the log

1. First: add to the home screen

So it works on a summit without signal, open it once beforehand while you are online and add it to the home screen.

  • iPhone: open in Safari, then Share → "Add to Home Screen".
  • Android: Chrome menu → "Add to Home screen" (or "Add to home screen" in the app menu).

After the first visit it works offline, and your log is saved on the device automatically. Only the summit/park suggestions and sync need a connection.

2. Before you leave (PC): settings and plans

Open https://fleonline.sotalive.net/ in a PC browser.

Settings

The gear at the top right opens the settings.

  • Frequently used rigs: one rig/antenna per line. When you type rig in the log, these appear as suggestions (e.g. KX2+EFHW). If you use HAMLOG, write the names you defined in HAMLOG.
  • Time zone: the time zone the times in the log are written in (UTC, JST, ...). One log uses one time zone, and a timezone line goes at the top of the log (none for UTC).
    • If you change it when the log has QSOs, you are asked whether to convert the times. Convert rewrites each time as the same moment in the new zone (fixing the date when a time crosses midnight). Keep the times only rewrites the timezone line (when you wrote in JST but forgot the timezone line). Cancel changes nothing. After a rewrite, "Undo" in the notice brings the log back.
    • SOTA, POTA and WWFF files are always in UTC. The HAMLOG file follows the log's time zone (J for JST; any other zone goes out in UTC as U).
  • Insert the current time: confirming a QSO line without a time inserts the current time (on by default). Turn it off when typing in a paper log; a line without a time then takes the time of the line before. This is set per device and is not synced.
  • What confirming writes: whether confirming with the green bar also writes band/mode and RST. The RST setting is kept per layout; in the compact layout it is off at first (the log stays as you typed it, and the green bar shows the filled-in values).
  • Screen layout: "Classic", "Compact (trial)" or "Full keys (trial)". Compact keeps the line you are typing, the green bar and the digit keys together (see Compact layout). Full keys also shows an on-screen keyboard instead of the phone's (see Full keys layout; available on touch devices such as phones). A phone starts with full keys the first time, a PC with classic. This is set per device and is not synced.

Plans (destinations)

Menu ("…") → "Plans" → "Add a plan" to register the summit you are going to in advance.

Namee.g. Mt. Takao 10/25 (your own label)
DateOptional. The day of the activation
My callsigne.g. JL1NIE/1
SOTA / POTA / WWFFType a name or number and suggestions appear (TK-0, ...). Just pick the summit or park
GridOptional. The locate button fills it in too
Rig, QSL messageOptional. The rig goes to HAMLOG's Rig=. The QSL message can use $mysota and $rig

You can register up to 50 plans. On the day, you only pick one.

3. Sync the PC and the phone

Settings and plans entered on the PC can be synced to your phone. The log text is not synced (a log written on the phone stays on the phone).

On the PC

  1. Gear → "Sync settings and plans" → "Start syncing (make a code)".
  2. A QR code and a 26-character sync code appear.

On the phone

  • Android: scan the QR with the camera (e.g. Google Lens) and press "Join" on the page that opens.
  • iPhone: Safari and the home-screen app have separate storage. Open the home-screen app, then Gear → "Scan QR" and scan the PC's QR, or type the sync code and press "Join".

After joining once, syncing is automatic. Plans you add on the PC arrive the next time you open the phone app with a connection. On the summit, synced plans are available even without signal.

Anyone who has the sync code can read and write your settings and plans, so do not share the QR or the code. To stop, use "Stop syncing" in the settings (you can also erase the server's copy).

4. On the summit: write the log

First: choose a plan

  1. Menu → "New log".
  2. Under "Choose a plan", tap today's plan. Your callsign, summit, grid and so on are filled in.
  3. Press "Write to log". Lines like these are written at the top of the log:
mycall JL1NIE/1
mysota JA/TK-001
date 2026-10-25
rig KX2+EFHW
qslmsg $mysota $rig

You can also skip plans and type your callsign and summit on the spot (suggestions appear as you type a name).

Write QSOs

Write one QSO per line. First write the band and mode on one line.

40m cw
0230 ja1abc
35 ja2xyz 579
41 jr1qrp/1 ja/st-012
ssb
52 ja3pqr
20m ssb
58 ja8abc
  • 0230 ja1abc: JA1ABC at 02:30. The time carries over from the previous line, so afterwards you only write the minutes (35 → 02:35).
  • Band and mode stay until you change them. To change only the mode write ssb; to change the band too, write 20m ssb on its own line.
  • 579: the report you sent. If omitted, the mode's default is used (CW 599 / SSB 59). Write two numbers (5 7) to add the report you received. In CW, 5 44 means 559 449 (the missing digits are filled in).
  • ja/st-012: the other station is on a summit (S2S). Their park (jp-1234) is written the same way.
  • pm85rl: the other station's grid, written after the callsign. It goes to GL in HAMLOG and GRIDSQUARE in ADIF.
  • Add a message or remark with <Yamada-san> or {QRP}.

Reading the screen

The candidate bar under the editor shows how the current line is understood.

OrangeYou are still typing. The callsign etc. is not yet in a form that can be confirmed. Pause, and the bar tells you what is missing.
GreenUnderstood. The bar shows the line with band, mode, time and report filled in. Tap ✓ at the right end to confirm and move to the next line. Tap the callsign, RST or time to move the cursor to its end in the editor, then fix it with ⌫ (each tap on the RST switches between received and sent; an RST or time that is not written is written first). To reach a character in the middle, swipe the digit row (see Fix a QSO).
RedSomething must be fixed, e.g. a missing band or mode.
(empty line)Shows the current time and the band and mode at that line. The next QSO gets these values.
(settings line)Shows what the line itself does not: the date after day + (with the weekday), the name of the summit or park for mysota etc. (when online), the message of qslmsg with its variables replaced, or the rig for rig ("No rig" for rig -). Lines with nothing to add (mycall, comments, ...) look like an empty line.

The number on the left is the QSOs on the day at the cursor (UTC), with the total (such as /10) under it. Choose "Total" under "QSO count" in the settings to show only the total. Tap it to see the QSO list and errors. Tapping an error jumps to that line.

  • Tap a suggestion shown at the bottom of the editor to insert a keyword, band or mode (in the compact layout, suggestions appear over the green bar).
  • Turn on "Digit key row" in the settings for a row of digits above the keyboard (handy for callsigns such as JA1ABC/1). Swipe the digit row left or right to move the cursor one character at a time (tap the editor to get near first).
  • If you move, use Menu → "New log" to switch the location for the QSOs from there on.
  • The QSO list can also be opened from Menu → "QSO list".
  • On a PC, Ctrl+Z (⌘Z on a Mac) undoes the last edit, in any layout, the same way as ↶ in full keys (including changes made by confirming or picking a suggestion).

Compact layout (trial)

Choose "Compact" under "Screen layout" in the settings to keep the line you are typing, the green bar, the digit keys and ⌫ together, so your eyes and fingers move less. You can switch back to the classic layout in the settings at any time (the log stays as it is).

│ 1111 jj0pic         │
│ 2107 ja1xyz 7▌      │ (typing)
[3/10][JA1XYZ 579/599][✓Confirm]
[1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8][9][0]
[/][-][←][→][  Space ][ ⌫ ]
[ the phone's keyboard ]
  • The log sits at the bottom of the editor, so the line you are typing is right above the green bar. The log text is the same size as the callsign in the green bar.
  • Turning a phone sideways shows "Please turn your phone upright" (your log stays). Opened from the home screen, Android keeps it upright.
  • The green bar is shorter: the callsign and RST in the same large text on the first row, and the time, band, mode etc. small on the second.
  • Two rows of digit keys, the second with / - ← → space and ⌫, so you can type a space or delete without reaching for the edge of the phone's keyboard.
    • Hold ⌫ to keep deleting.
    • ← → move the cursor one character. Swiping either row also moves the cursor (starting on ⌫ moves it without deleting).
  • The button at the right end moves to the next line. On a QSO line (green) it is ✓ Confirm; on a line that is not a QSO, such as 40m cw, it is "↵ Next". "Next" starts a new line at the end of the current one, the same as Enter.
  • Suggestions appear over the green bar. They never cover the button at the right end or the QSO count on the left, so you can move on while they are shown.
  • The QSO count on the left is display only (tapping it does not open the list; use Menu → "QSO list"). When activating POTA or SOTA, the lower number is the QSOs needed for an activation (/10 for POTA, /4 for SOTA), and it turns green once the activation is complete. It turns red when there are errors, with a count such as ⚠2.
  • Confirming keeps the log as you typed it (writing the RST is off at first). Type 7 and the green bar shows the filled-in value such as 579/599. To write it into the log, turn on RST under "What confirming writes" in the settings while the compact or full keys layout is selected (this setting is kept separately from the classic layout, and the settings screen says which layout it is for).
  • While the keyboard is shown, there is no gap between the digit keys and the keyboard.

Full keys layout (trial)

Choose "Full keys" under "Screen layout" in the settings to get an on-screen keyboard, with just the keys needed to write FLE, instead of the phone's keyboard. No more switching the phone's keyboard between input modes. Everything else is the same as the compact layout (the log at the bottom, suggestions, the QSO count, and confirming keeps the log as you typed it).

[1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8][9][0]
[Q][W][E][R][T][Y][U][I][O][P]
[A][S][D][F][G][H][J][K][L][-]
 [Z][X][C][V][B][N][M][/][ ⌫ ]
[🌐][↶][←][Space][.][→][✓Confirm]
  • Tapping the editor does not bring up the phone's keyboard (the cursor still shows).
  • The keys are square and show capital letters, but letters go into the log in lowercase (FLE does not care about case). - is right of L and / is right of M, as on a PC keyboard.
  • . is right of space (for frequencies such as 50.230). Tap - twice quickly for , (the small , at the top right of the key).
  • ↶ undoes the last edit. If you deleted too much with ⌫, one tap brings back everything from before you started deleting (even across lines). Typing is undone one line at a time. Each tap goes further back.
  • The key at the bottom right moves to the next line: "✓ Confirm" on a QSO line (green), "↵ Next" on a line such as 40m cw. With full keys, the green bar has no button at its right end (this key is the only one).
  • The key you are pressing shows in a balloon above your finger. Tapping the gap between keys still types the nearest key.
  • Drag the handle on the top edge of the keyboard up or down to change the key height (from square up to 1.5 times). Taller keys are easier to hit, for example with gloves. The log uses the rest of the height. Each device remembers it.
  • Hold ⌫ to keep deleting. Swipe left or right from space, ← or → to move the cursor one character at a time (see Swipe). Letter keys never turn into a swipe, even if your finger slides while typing fast.
  • For Japanese text (messages such as <Yamada-san>, remarks, qslmsg), tap 🌐 to switch to the phone's keyboard. When done, tap "Back to full keys" at the bottom.

Fix a QSO

If the other station corrects a report or a callsign during the QSO, tap that part in the green candidate bar. The cursor in the editor moves to the end of that part, so delete from the end with ⌫ and type again (nothing is selected, so typing never erases other text).

TapWhere the cursor goes (|)
Callsign1005 ja1xyz| 57 599
RST (first tap)1005 ja1xyz 57 599| the report you received (the one usually corrected)
RST (tap again right away)1005 ja1xyz 57| 599 the report you sent
Time1005| ja1xyz 57 599
  • Example: you received 579, not 599 → tap the RST → ⌫ ⌫ → 79.
  • Example: it was JA1XYW, not JA1XYZ → tap the callsign → ⌫ → w.
  • If the RST or time was left out (the bar shows the filled-in value), tapping it writes that value into the line first, then moves to its end. On the line ja1xyz, tapping the RST gives ja1xyz 599 599|.
  • To fix a character in the middle, tap the part to get to its end, then swipe the digit row left or right to move the cursor (next section).
  • To fix an earlier QSO, tap its line in the editor. The line appears in the green bar and can be fixed the same way. For lines with errors, tap the QSO count on the left to see the list and tap an error to jump to its line (in the compact layout, use Menu → "QSO list").
  • Only ✓ at the right end of the green bar confirms (moves to the next line). Tapping a part never confirms. (In the compact layout, lines that are not QSOs also get "Next" at the right end.)

Swipe the digit row to move the cursor

You can move the cursor one character at a time without aiming your finger between characters. Lines other than QSOs (date, mysota, qslmsg, comments, ...) have no parts to tap in the green bar, so this is the main way to fix them. On QSO lines, use it to fix a character in the middle of a part.

[1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8][9][0][/][-]
 ← keep your finger down, slide →

In the compact layout, you can swipe from anywhere on either row of digit keys. For a single character, you can also tap ← or →.

  1. Put a finger anywhere on the digit row (any key will do).
  2. Without lifting it, slide left or right. The cursor moves one character per about 20px in that direction, across parts and lines.
  3. Lift the finger to stop. A swipe never types a digit.
  • If you lift almost without moving (less than 10px), it is a normal tap and types that digit.
  • Example: change 1151 to 1152 in mypota jp-1151 jp-1164 → tap near 1151 in the editor → swipe the digit row to 1151| → ⌫ → 2.
  • Example: change date 2026-10-19 to the 18th → tap the end of the line (19|) → ⌫ → 8. No swipe is needed at the end of a line.
  • Example: change the middle 5 of the sent report 559 to 7 → tap the RST twice for 559| → swipe the digit row left by one character for 55|9 → ⌫ → 7, giving 579.
  • Tap the editor to get near, then swipe for the fine position.
  • Show the digit row with "Digit key row" in the settings (it is on from the start on touch devices).

5. After operating: make the files

  1. Menu ("…") → "Export (ADIF etc.)".
  2. If something is missing (callsign, date, location, grid, ...), a check screen appears. Choose to write the missing items to the log and export, or export anyway.
  3. A zip file is downloaded. Its name has the date of the first QSO, the locations and the time of export (such as fle-20261019@JA-ST-012-JP-1151-1543.zip), so exporting again never overwrites it. "Save FLE log" uses the same name with .txt.
sotaYYYYMMDD.csvSOTA log (QSOs from a summit, per day). sota-s2s-….csv too if there are S2S contacts
CALLSIGN@PARK-DATE.adiPOTA ADIF (per park and day). WWFF is the same
hamlog-….csvCSV for importing into HAMLOG (with QSL message and rig)

Import the sota….csv into the SOTA database, the .adi file into the POTA site, and the hamlog….csv into HAMLOG. If any line has an error, no files are made; only a text file listing the errors is produced (fix them and export again).

To convert files from other logging software, use logconv2.

To keep the FLE log itself, use Menu → "Save FLE log" to save it as a text file. "Open FLE log" reads it back.

6. FLE syntax (reference)

The log is line-based. Blank lines are ignored, and # starts a comment to the end of the line.

Directive lines (effective from that line on)

mycall JL1NIE/1Your callsign
operator JL1NIEThe operator (defaults to your callsign)
mysota JA/TK-001Your summit. - clears it
mypota JP-0001 JP-0002Your parks (list overlapping parks). - clears it
mywwff JAFF-0001Your WWFF reference
mygrid PM95uqYour grid (4, 6 or 8 characters)
date 2026-10-25The date (10/25 also works). Resets the time to 00:00
day + / day ++Advance the date by 1 / 2 days (when crossing midnight)
timezone jstTime zone of the times: jst, utc, or a number such as +9. Default UTC. Normally the "Time zone" setting writes it at the top
rig KX2+EFHWThe rig (HAMLOG's Rig=). - clears it
qslmsg …QSL message. Can use $mysota $mypota $mywwff $rig

QSO lines

List these words in any order.

Time1–4 digits. 1 digit = minute units, 2 = minutes, 3 = H:MM, 4 = HH:MM. Carries over from the previous line
Band40m 2m 70cm etc. Stays until the next band
Frequency7.030 (MHz). Also sets the band
Modecw ssb fm am ft8 etc. Stays until the next mode
CallsignOne per line. This is what creates the QSO
ReportNumbers after the callsign. One = sent, two = sent and received. Short forms are completed to suit the mode. CW etc.: 1 digit = 5□9 (7 → 579), 2 digits = □□9 (44 → 449). SSB/FM/AM: 1 digit = 5□ (7 → 57), 2 digits as written. 3 digits are kept in any mode. For FT8 etc., an SNR such as -18
Their summit/parkja/st-012 (SOTA), jp-1234 (POTA), jaff-0001 (WWFF)
Messages<…> QSO message, […] QSL message, {…} remark

Common errors

  • Missing band / mode: write a line such as 40m cw before the callsign.
  • "rigset was replaced by rig": rewrite an old rigset line as rig KX2+EFHW.
  • Callsign shape: write it in the international form, including ones that start with a digit (7K1ABC).
