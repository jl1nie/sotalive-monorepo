1. About this app

FLEonline lets you write your operating log on a summit or in a park, on the spot, with just a phone. The log uses a short notation called FLE (Fast Log Entry): after 40m cw you write 0230 ja1abc, then 35 ja2xyz, leaving out whatever is the same as the line before. You can write without a signal on the summit, and when you are back you can make the SOTA, POTA, WWFF and HAMLOG files in one go.

What you can do

WriteOne QSO per line in FLE. The candidate bar below the log area shows the line with the omitted time, band, mode and RST filled in. On a phone you type on an on-screen keyboard with only the keys FLE needs (full keys)
Without a signalOnce you have opened the app, you can write without a signal. The log is saved on the device automatically
PrepareRegister where you are going as a "plan", and just choose it on the day. The summit map shows the activation zone, the municipality (JCC/JCG) and the POTA parks
Sync with a PCSettings and plans can be synced between a PC and a phone
Make the filesMakes the SOTA CSV, the POTA, WWFF and SOTA ADIF files and the HAMLOG CSV in one zip. You can also add an ADIF file from other logging software to the log

What you cannot do, and notes

  • The log itself is not synced. A log written on the phone exists only on the phone.
  • The app does not upload anything to SOTA, POTA or other sites. You upload the files it makes yourself.
  • Searching for summits and parks, looking up JCC/JCG, and syncing need a signal.

2. First-time setup

Add the app to the home screen (optional)

Adding it to the home screen is not required. You can keep using it in a browser tab (if you close the tab, reopen it from the history and the log is still there). Adding it lets you open it from an icon and gives you more screen space. Either way, if you will use it on a summit without a signal, open it once where you have a signal. To add it:

  • iPhone: open https://fleonline.sotalive.net/ in Safari, then the share button → "Add to Home Screen".
  • Android: the Chrome menu → "Add to Home screen" (or "Add to home screen" in this app's menu).

From then on, open it from the home screen icon. On an iPhone, Safari and the home-screen app keep their data separately. Use the home-screen app for your log, settings and plans.

Settings to decide first

The gear at the top right opens the settings. Most can stay as they are. Check just these three first (for all settings, see "Settings").

Time zoneThe time zone you write the log's times in. It starts as UTC. To write in Japan time, choose JST. When it is not UTC, a timezone line is written at the top of the log
Frequently used rigsOne per line (e.g. KX2+EFHW). They are offered as candidates when you type rig in the log. If you use HAMLOG, write the names defined in HAMLOG
HAMLOG time / outputOnly if you use HAMLOG. Whether the CSV times are written in JST (J, the default) or UTC (U), and what goes into QTH, Rmks1 and Rmks2

If you also use a PC

Registering plans is easier on a PC's large screen. Settings and plans entered on a PC can be copied to the phone by syncing. Everything can also be done on the phone alone.

3. The day before: prepare

This manual follows an ordinary SOTA activation. Differences for POTA and WWFF are noted where they come up.

Register a plan

Menu ("…") → "Plans" → "Add a plan" to register where you are going.

Namee.g. Mt. Jimba 10/25 (a name for yourself)
DateOptional. The day you operate
CallsignYour callsign (e.g. JL1NIE/1)
SOTA / POTA / WWFFType a name or a number to get candidates (陣馬, KN-02, …). Just choose one
GridOptional. The "Here" button fills it in too
Rig / QSL messageOptional. The rig goes into HAMLOG's Rig=. The QSL message can use $mysota, $rig and so on
MemoOptional. JCC/JCG and the like. When you choose a summit or park, it is looked up if you have a signal

POTA: where parks overlap, list them in the POTA field (JP-1151 JP-1164).

Check the summit map

The "Map" button next to the plan's SOTA field shows the summit's activation zone, municipal borders and the POTA parks in each municipality. For a summit on a prefecture border, decide here which side you will operate from (the JCC/JCG for the memo and the POTA park). See "Summit map" for details.

Check before you leave

  • Is the map saved on the device? The maps of summits in your plans are saved automatically while you have a signal. If the plan list shows "Map: saved", you can see it on the summit without a signal. If it shows "not saved yet", open the app where you have a signal.
  • If you entered the plan on a PC: open the app on the phone where you have a signal and check that the plan has arrived (syncing happens when you open the app).
  • It is reassuring to check that the app opens from the home screen.

4. On the day: write the log

Start a new log

  1. Menu → "New log". Your callsign is filled in with the one you used last.
  2. Under "Choose a plan", tap today's plan. The callsign, summit, grid, memo and so on are filled in.
  3. If you like, also enter the band (or frequency) and mode.
  4. Tap "Write to log". The log you were writing is replaced by a new log that starts with lines like these.
mycall JL1NIE/1
mysota JA/KN-028
date 2026-10-25
rig KX2+EFHW
qslmsg $mysota $rig
# JCC#111001 相模原市緑区
40m cw

The log you replaced can be brought back with "Undo" in the menu. A line starting with # is a memo (JCC/JCG and the like) and does not go into the exported files. You can also enter your callsign and summit on the spot without a plan.

Check that you are in the activation zone

Before you start making QSOs, open Menu → "Summit map". If your position (GPS) is in the activation zone, the summit's code is shown in green below the map. If you are in a park, the park's code is shown too. In the list below it, the municipality you are in (the JCC/JCG for the memo) is marked with ▶. On a summit on a border, like Mt. Jimba, check here which side you are on.

Write QSOs

Write one QSO per line.

40m cw
0230 ja1abc
35 ja2xyz 579
41 jr1qrp/1 st-012
ssb
52 ja3pqr
  • 0230 ja1abc: JA1ABC at 02:30. The hour carries over from the line before, so after that you only need the minutes (35 → 02:35).
  • The time is filled in automatically (the setting "Insert the current time", on by default). If you type only the callsign and press OK, the time you typed the line's first character is written.
  • 579: the report you sent. If you leave it out, the mode's standard report (CW 599 / SSB 59) is used.
  • st-012: the other station is on a summit (S2S). The association (ja/) can be left out when it is the same as your summit's. Write the other station's park (jp-1234, P2P) the same way.
  • The band and mode stay until you change them (ssb, 20m ssb).

For the full syntax, see "FLE syntax".

Confirm and go to the next line (OK)

When the candidate bar below the log area turns green, the line has been read as a QSO. Press the ✓ OK at the right end to add the time and so on, and go to the next line.

  • A QSO is in the log as soon as it is written correctly, even without pressing OK. But a line without a time takes the time of the line before. To write the current time, press OK.
  • The keyboard's ↵ only starts a new line; it does not write the time.
  • An orange candidate bar means the line is still being typed; red means something needs fixing ("Reading the screen").

Fix on the spot

If the other station repeats a report or callsign, tap the part to fix (call, RST or time) on the candidate bar. The cursor in the log area moves to the end of that part, so delete with ⌫ and type it again. Tapping RST once moves to the report you received; tapping it again moves to the report you sent. If you delete too much with ⌫, the keyboard's ↶ brings it back. See "Fixing a QSO" for details.

Watch the QSO count

The number at the left of the candidate bar is the day's QSO count. The small number below it is the QSO count needed for a valid activation (SOTA /4, POTA /10), and it turns green when you reach it.

When you move to another location

When you move to another summit or park, put the cursor on the line where you will write the first QSO at the new place, and open Menu → "Change location". The location of the QSOs from that line on changes (it stays the same log).

When you pass midnight

If the time is being filled in automatically and the date changes, a day + line is added automatically when you press OK, and a notice appears at the bottom of the screen.

If you type the times yourself, and the previous QSO is at 21:00 or later and this line's time is before 03:00 (0002 after 2358, for example), pressing OK adds day + and shows "New day" above the log for about 3 seconds.

In both cases, pressing "Undo" while it is shown cancels it.

When you go around calling other activators

When you call stations operating from other summits or parks from your summit (S2S, P2P), also write the other station's summit or park, as in ja1abc tk-001.

A line with the other station's summit or park takes the time you press OK, not the time you started typing the line. You start writing after hearing the other station and then wait your turn to call, so the time you started typing can be far from the actual QSO (the setting "Chase, S2S and P2P lines take the time you press OK", on by default).

A line where an S2S station calls you while you are calling CQ also takes the time you press OK, because you write the other station's summit. Such QSOs are short, so the difference stays small.

5. Afterwards: make the files

  1. Menu → "Export (ADIF etc.)".
  2. If the date, your callsign or the location is missing, a check screen appears. Choose "Write to log and export" to write the missing items before exporting, or "Export anyway" (the grid is optional, so a missing grid does not bring up the screen).
  3. A zip file is downloaded.

Upload the files in the zip to each site or program.

sotaYYYYMMDD.csvUpload to the SOTA database
callsign@park-date.adiUpload to the POTA site (one per park)
callsign@summit-date.adiThe SOTA ADIF (one per summit; / in the name becomes -). Use it when you upload to SOTA as ADIF (the CSV also works). The WWFF ADIF has the same form
hamlog-….csvImport into HAMLOG
fle-….txtThe original FLE log, the same as "Save FLE log". Keep it

For what the files contain, see "Exported files". If some lines have errors, the files are not made; the zip holds only a text describing the errors and the original FLE log. Fix them and export again.

If the device freezes while you are logging

The log is saved automatically inside the device (the browser's storage) as you type: about 0.4 s after your last keystroke, and when you close the app or switch to another app. You do not need to do anything.

  • If the phone or PC freezes or the battery dies, everything up to the last save remains. Characters typed in the last 0.4 s or so may be lost. If the device stops suddenly before the browser has finished writing, a few earlier lines may be lost too.
  • When you reopen the browser, the previous log is there. It remains when you reopen from the history, too, in the same browser, and a closed tab is the same.
  • If you clear the browser's browsing data or site data, the log is deleted too. On an iPhone, Safari and the home-screen app keep separate storage (see "Initial setup").
  • To be safe, save the log to a file with Menu → "Save FLE log" at a good stopping point, even on the summit.

Save the FLE log

The FLE log itself can be saved as a text file with Menu → "Save FLE log". You can read it back later with "Open FLE log". The exported zip also holds the same file.

Add ADIF from other logging software

If part of the same operation was logged in other software, Menu → "Add ADIF" turns that ADIF into FLE lines and adds them to the end of the log. The log before adding can be brought back with "Undo".

If you used the wrong time zone

If, for example, you wrote in JST but the setting was UTC, change the time zone in the settings. For a log with QSOs, the app asks whether to Convert (rewrite the times to the same moments in the new time zone) or Keep the times (rewrite only the timezone line). If you wrote in JST but forgot the timezone line, choose "Keep the times".

6. Screens, settings and syntax

Reading the screen

The candidate bar below the log area shows how the line at the cursor is read. Its colour shows the state.

OrangeStill being typed. The callsign or another part is not yet in a form that can be confirmed. When you stop typing, what is missing is shown.
GreenRead successfully. The line is shown with the time, band, mode and reports filled in. The ✓ OK at the right adds the time and so on, and goes to the next line. On the last line it adds a new line; in the middle of the log it just moves to the next line (no line is added). Tapping the call, RST or time moves the cursor in the log area to the end of that part ("Fixing a QSO").
RedSomething needs fixing, such as a missing band or mode.
(empty line)Shows the current time and the band and mode at that line. The next QSO takes these values.
(directive line)Shows what the line does not show by itself: for day +, the date it moves to (with the weekday); for mysota and the like, the name of the summit or park (when you have a signal); for qslmsg, the message with the variables filled in; for rig, the rig (rig - shows "no rig").
  • QSO count: at the left of the candidate bar, the QSO count for the day at the cursor (UTC date) is shown large and the total small. For SOTA and POTA, the small number becomes the QSO count needed for a valid activation (SOTA /4, POTA /10) and turns green when reached. If some lines have errors it turns red and shows the number of errors, like ⚠2. The "QSO count" setting can show only the total.
  • Time and location: above the log area, the time, your callsign and the summit, WWFF and parks valid at the cursor's line are shown large. Below them is the memo valid at the cursor's line (the last # line before it). The time is shown in the same time zone as the times written to the log, with UTC added when it is not UTC. When the screen is too narrow (for example, while the phone's keyboard is shown), it is not shown.
  • Candidates: while you type, candidates for keywords, bands, modes and so on appear over the candidate bar. Tap one to enter it.
  • The log is aligned to the bottom, so the line you are typing sits just above the candidate bar.
  • If you turn the phone sideways, "Please turn your phone upright" is shown (the log stays as it is).

The phone keyboard (full keys)

On a phone or another touch device, instead of the phone's own keyboard, an on-screen keyboard with only the keys FLE needs (full keys) appears. You do not need to switch from kana input to letters.

      10:05:12 JST  UTC 01:05
       JA1ABC  JA/KN-028
│ 1111 jj0pic         │
│ 2107 ja1xyz 7▌      │ (the line you are typing)
[3/10][JA1XYZ 579/599   ][✓]
[1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8][9][0]
[Q][W][E][R][T][Y][U][I][O][P]
[A][S][D][F][G][H][J][K][L][-]
[↑][Z][X][C][V][B][N][M][/][⌫]
[🌐][↶][←][            ][→][ ↵ ]
  • Tapping the log area does not bring up the phone's keyboard (the cursor does appear).
  • Letters go into the log in lower case (FLE does not distinguish upper and lower case). - is to the right of L, and / to the right of M.
  • Pressing a key twice in a row gives another character: / twice gives . (for frequencies like 7.0295), and - twice gives ,. They are shown small at the bottom right of the key.
  • A long press (about 0.5 s) enters another symbol: 3 gives # (for memo lines and JCC#), 0 gives + (for day +), and 7 gives ? (for characters you could not copy; type JA1AB? and the app reminds you before exporting). The bubble while you press changes to the symbol, and it is entered when you release.
  • The key left of Z is Caps (↑). Pressing it fills in the ↑, and letters are entered in capitals. Useful when lower-case m and n are hard to tell apart. The state is remembered on each device.
  • The widest key in the bottom row is the space, and the bottom right is ↵ (they have no labels).
  • ↵ only starts a new line and does not confirm (it does not write the time). In the middle of a line, it splits the line there. In the middle of the log, it lets you add a QSO in between. To confirm, use the ✓ OK on the candidate bar.
  • ↶ undoes the last edit ("Undo").
  • Holding ⌫ keeps deleting.
  • Swiping left or right on the space key or the ← → keys moves the cursor one character at a time ("Moving the cursor by swiping").
  • The key height can be changed by dragging the handle on the keyboard's top edge up or down (from the height where the keys are square, up to 1.5 times that). Useful when wearing gloves. It is remembered on each device.
  • Japanese (messages like <山田さん> or qslmsg) is typed by switching to the phone's keyboard with 🌐. When you are done, press "Back to full keys" at the bottom.
  • On screens such as "New log", "Change location" and "Plans", the callsign, SOTA, POTA, WWFF, grid and memo fields are typed with the same keyboard (letters in capitals from the start). ↵ moves to the next field. To write Japanese in the memo (such as a municipality name), switch to the phone's keyboard with 🌐. The plan name uses the phone's keyboard from the start.

Using a PC

On a PC you type on the physical keyboard (the full keys do not appear). The differences from a phone are:

  • Ctrl+Z (⌘Z on a Mac) undoes the last edit, the same way as ↶ on the full keys.
  • Candidates appear over the bottom edge of the log area.
  • Clicking the QSO count at the left of the candidate bar shows a list of QSOs and errors. Clicking an error moves to that line (on a phone, use Menu → "QSO list").
  • Turning on the "Digit key row" setting shows digit keys below the log area (making it easier to type the digits of callsigns and / -). Swiping on them moves the cursor.
  • Whether confirming writes the RST is set separately for the PC and the phone (on a PC it is written from the start; on a phone it is not written at first).

Fixing a QSO

Tap the part you want to fix on the candidate bar. The cursor in the log area moves to the end of that part, so delete from the end with ⌫ and type it again (nothing is selected, so typing does not erase other characters).

What you tapWhere the cursor goes (|)
Call1005 ja1xyz| 57 599
RST (first tap)1005 ja1xyz 57 599| the report you received (usually the one repeated)
RST (tap again)1005 ja1xyz 57| 599 the report you sent
Time1005| ja1xyz 57 599
  • Example: the report you received was 579, not 599 → tap RST → ⌫ ⌫ → 79.
  • Example: it was JA1XYW, not JA1XYZ → tap the call → ⌫ → w.
  • Tapping an RST or time you left out (the bar shows the filled-in value) writes that value into the line first and then moves to its end. Tapping RST on the line ja1xyz gives ja1xyz 599 599|.
  • For a character in the middle, tap the part to move to its end, then swipe to position the cursor.
  • To fix an earlier QSO, tap that line in the log area. The line appears on the candidate bar, and you can fix it the same way.
  • Tapping a part does not move to the next line.

Moving the cursor by swiping

You can move the cursor one character at a time without aiming your finger between characters. Lines that are not QSOs, such as date, mysota and qslmsg, cannot be fixed by tapping parts of the candidate bar, so this is the main way to fix them.

  1. Put your finger on the space key or the ← → keys on a phone, or on the digit row on a PC.
  2. Without lifting it, swipe left or right. The cursor moves one character for about every 20 px, in the direction you swipe. It moves across lines and parts too.
  3. Lift your finger to stop. When you swipe, the character of the key you started on (space or digit) is not entered.
  • For a single character, pressing ← or → also moves the cursor.
  • Example: change 1151 to 1152 in mypota jp-1151 jp-1164 → tap around 1151 → swipe to 1151| → ⌫ → 2.
  • Example: change the middle 5 of the sent report 559 to 7 → tap RST twice to get 559| → swipe left one character to 55|9 → ⌫ → 7.
  • It is easiest to tap the log area to get near the spot and then swipe for the fine positioning.

Plans

Menu → "Plans". You can register up to 50 places you are going to. The fields are as in the table in "The day before: prepare".

  • Use them with "Choose a plan" on the "New log" and "Change location" screens. The plan's callsign, summit, parks, grid and memo are filled in, and when you write, the plan's date, rig and QSL message also become directive lines (not written if the same lines are already in the log).
  • The maps of summits in plans are saved on the device automatically while you have a signal. The list shows "Map: saved" or "not saved yet".
  • Plans are synced.

The location screen and memos

A memo and remarks are different things. A memo is a line starting with #; it is a note that applies to the rest of the operation after that line (JCC/JCG and the like; it does not go into the exported files). Remarks are written as {…} inside a QSO line and apply to that one QSO only (the other station's name, "a neighbour" and so on; they go into the remarks of the ADIF and HAMLOG).

This screen opens from "New log" and "Change location". Enter your callsign, SOTA, POTA, WWFF, grid and memo, and "Write to log" writes the directive lines (mycall, mysota and so on).

  • SOTA, POTA and WWFF offer candidates when you type a name or number (a signal is needed). "Map" opens the summit map. The grid can also be filled in with "Here".
  • If something is not in a valid form, the screen stays open and the problem is shown below the field.
  • A memo is written as a line starting with #, so it does not go into the exported files (it can go into HAMLOG's columns by setting). Write JCC/JCG and the like. When you choose a summit or park from the candidates, or get your position with "Here", JCC/JCG is looked up and filled in if you have a signal (using data from the Geospatial Information Authority of Japan and this app's server). A memo you typed yourself is not overwritten.
  • If you write with the memo field left empty, it is looked up from the summit (or the first park) and written. If it cannot be looked up, nothing is written.
  • When the screen opens, the memo field holds the memo valid at that point. If you change it and write, a new # line is added (the earlier memo is not removed). If it is the same, nothing is written.
  • On "New log", you can also enter the band (or frequency) and mode. They are written after the directive lines, before the first QSO.
  • When you write a location, your callsign is remembered and becomes the initial value for the next "New log".

Syncing a PC and a phone

Settings (those marked "yes" in the Settings table) and plans are synced between devices. The log itself is not synced.

  1. On the first device, such as a PC: gear → "Sync settings and plans" → "Start syncing (make a code)". A QR code and a 26-character sync code appear.
  2. On the phone:
    • Android: scan the QR with the camera (Google Lens or similar) → "Join" on the screen that opens.
    • iPhone: open the home-screen app, then gear → "Scan QR" to scan the QR, or enter the sync code and tap "Join" (scanning with the camera opens Safari, which does not reach the home-screen app).
  • When you join, the device's settings are replaced by the synced settings. The device's plans stay and are merged with the synced plans. The log does not change.
  • Once you have joined, syncing happens automatically when you open the app (where you have a signal). On the summit, synced plans work without a signal.
  • To stop, use "Stop syncing" (you can also delete the settings and plans on the server).

Anyone who knows the sync code can read and write your settings and plans. Do not show the QR or the code to others.

Summit map

Open it with Menu → "Summit map" (for the summit valid at the cursor), or with "Map" on the plan and location screens.

  • Shows the summit's activation zone, municipal borders and the POTA parks in each municipality.
  • Overlays your position (GPS) on the map. Below the map, the summit or park codes that apply where you are (the summit's code if you are in the activation zone, a park's code if you are in the park) are shown in green. Near a border (within the GPS error and the error of the area data), it does not commit and shows them in orange. Outside the zone, it shows the distance to the summit. The GPS accuracy is shown too.
  • Below that, so you can read them out when asked during a QSO, it lists the summit's code, name and height, and for each municipality the zone touches, its JCC/JCG and the POTA parks there. The municipality you are in is marked with ▶ and listed first.
  • A park marked "(partly)" covers only part of the zone within that municipality (the hatched area on the map; the list shows a mark in the same colour).
  • Maps of summits in plans and of summits you wrote as your location are saved on the device while you have a signal, and can be seen without a signal.

Exported files

The zip is named after the first QSO's date, the location and the time you exported (such as fle-20261019@JA-ST-012-JP-1151-1543.zip; each export gets a different name, so nothing is overwritten). "Save FLE log" uses the same name with .txt.

sotaYYYYMMDD.csvThe SOTA log (QSOs on a summit, one file per UTC date). With S2S QSOs, also sota-s2s-…csv
callsign@park-date.adiThe POTA ADIF (one per park; a single file even across dates, named after the first QSO's date). WWFF and SOTA (one per summit; / in the name becomes -) have the same form. FREQ is included when you wrote a frequency, and MY_SOTA_REF (yours) and SOTA_REF (the other station's) when there is a summit
hamlog-….csvA CSV for importing into HAMLOG (times per the "HAMLOG time" setting; QTH, Rmks1 and Rmks2 per the "HAMLOG output" setting)
contest-date.txtA file in ZLOG format, made for contest logs
fle-date@location-time.txtThe original FLE log. Its name and contents are the same as "Save FLE log"
  • SOTA, POTA and WWFF files are always in UTC.
  • If some lines have errors, these files are not made; the zip holds only a text describing the errors and the original FLE log.
  • If the date, your callsign or the location is missing, or a callsign contains ? for a character you could not copy, a check screen appears before exporting.

FLE syntax

The log is line-based. Empty lines are ignored. Everything from # to the end of the line is a memo and does not go into the output.

Directive lines (valid from that line on)

mycall JL1NIE/1Your callsign
operator JL1NIEOperator (taken from your callsign if omitted)
mysota JA/KN-028Your summit. - clears it
mypota JP-0001 JP-0002Your parks (list overlapping parks). - clears them
mywwff JAFF-0001Your WWFF reference
mygrid PM95uqYour grid locator (4, 6 or 8 characters)
date 2026-10-25Date (10/25 also works). The time goes back to 00:00
day + / day ++Moves the date forward 1 / 2 days (when passing midnight). For when it is added automatically, see "When you pass midnight"
timezone jstThe time zone of the times: jst, utc, or a number like +9. UTC if omitted. Usually the "Time zone" setting writes it at the top. Changing it in the middle of the log reads the previous time as the same moment in the new time zone (timezone jst after UTC 23:30 continues from 08:30 the next day)
rig KX2+EFHWRig (HAMLOG's Rig=). - clears it
qslmsg …QSL message. Can use $mysota $mypota $mywwff $rig

QSO lines

Put the following words in any order.

Time1 to 4 digits: 1 digit = the last digit of the minutes, 2 = minutes, 3 = H:MM, 4 = HH:MM. The omitted upper digits carry over from the line before
Band40m 2m 70cm and so on. Stays until the next band
Frequency7.030 (MHz). Also sets the band
Modecw ssb fm am ft8 and so on. Stays until the next mode
CallsignOne per line. Each makes one QSO. A character you could not copy is ? (JA1AB?)
ReportNumbers after the callsign. One number is the report you sent; two are the report you sent and the report you received.
Omitted digits are filled in according to the mode (□ is the digit you typed).
CW etc.: 1 digit = 5□9 (7→579), 2 digits = □□9 (44→449)
SSB, FM, AM: 1 digit = 5□ (7→57), 2 digits = as typed
3 digits are used as typed in any mode. FT8 and similar use an SNR like -18
Other station's summit / parkja/st-012 or st-012 (SOTA; the association can be omitted only when you have your own summit), jp-1234 (POTA), jaff-0001 (WWFF)
Other station's gridpm85rl, written after the callsign. Goes into HAMLOG's GL and ADIF's GRIDSQUARE
Messages<…> QSO message, […] QSL message, {…} remarks
